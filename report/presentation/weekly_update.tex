\begin{frame}{This week, in one sentence}
  \begin{block}{}
    \centering\large
    I tried hard to \textbf{break} last week's ``we can't predict it'' result --- on \textbf{two new datasets}

  \end{block}
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item The ``no'' is now \textbf{triangulated}: same answer on a camera-trap dataset, an internet-video

    \item Everything below is built, hermetically tested, run on the GPU, committed, and written up.
  \end{itemize}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- where we were
\begin{frame}{Where we were last week}
  \begin{block}{}\centering
    On \textbf{SA-FARI} we can \emph{measure} SAM\,3's reliability but \textbf{cannot predict it in advance}
    from label-free ``distance'' signals --- beyond a small, obvious \textbf{animal-size} effect.
  \end{block}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item Clean negative result, defended by a positive control (the same test \emph{does} catch size).
    \item \textbf{The gap:} it was one model, one dataset, one inference run --- so not truly \emph{independent}
          evidence, and it left one before-running idea untried.
    \item \textbf{This week closes both.}
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- what I did

      \item \textbf{MammAlps} --- Alpine camera traps, 5 species, box-only ground truth.

  \end{enumerate}
  \vspace{0.2em}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- MammAlps
\begin{frame}{Replication 1 --- MammAlps (small, camera-trap)}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item Different continent, species, and camera rig; box-only, so I score box-overlap. \textbf{20 report
          cards, 5 species.}
    \item \textbf{Result: the distances predict nothing} --- same as SA-FARI.
    \item But it is a \textbf{consistency check, not proof}: at 20 cards the test is \textbf{underpowered} ---
          even a feature I \emph{know} is predictive can't pass it here.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{block}{The one ``significant'' feature was a fake}
    \footnotesize
    Visual distance cleared the bar --- but pointing the \textbf{opposite way} to the theory (more-unusual
    animals detected \emph{better}) and driven by animal size. The \textbf{same confound} SA-FARI showed, not a
    finding.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- BURST
\begin{frame}{Replication 2 --- BURST (bigger, mask-native, many species)}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item \textbf{41 species, 132 report cards}, real masks, genuine ``following'' content --- the powered test
          MammAlps couldn't be. SAM\,3 works here (score $\approx 0.64$).
    \item \textbf{Result: a third convergent null} --- the distances still predict nothing.
    \item Far better powered than the tiny MammAlps by sheer numbers --- but a \textbf{convergent} null, not
          proof: no before-running signal fires here (not even animal size), so a small effect can't be ruled out.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{block}{}\centering\footnotesize
    Three datasets now agree. The ``no'' is not a quirk of camera-trap data or of one inference run.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- cross-dataset
\begin{frame}{The picture across all three datasets}
  \centering
  \footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    Predict SAM\,3's score from\dots & SA-FARI & MammAlps & BURST \\
    \midrule
    the four before-running distances & \no\ no & \no\ no & \no\ no \\
    \textbf{animal size} (the confound) & \ck\ a little & \no\ no & \no\ no \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item No \emph{before-running} label-free distance predicts transfer, anywhere.
    \item The \emph{only} thing that ever reaches significance is the \textbf{animal-size} confound --- modestly,
          and only on SA-FARI --- which doubles as the \textbf{positive control}: the same test that stays flat
          for the distances \emph{does} catch size out-of-sample, so the ``no'' is real, not a weak test.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- familiarity idea
\begin{frame}{The last idea: ask SAM\,3 how well it ``knows'' the animal}
  \begin{block}{The hole it plugs}
    \centering\footnotesize
    ``Novelty'' has always meant distance from \emph{our} pile --- but \textbf{nobody knows what SAM\,3 was
    actually trained on.} So maybe our distances point at the wrong reference.
  \end{block}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item \textbf{The trick:} instead of a pile we picked, ask the model. Push each animal through \textbf{SAM\,3's
          own eyes} and see how distinctly it sits in its feature space.
    \item Familiar animal $\to$ a clean, distinct blob; genuinely new $\to$ fuzzy, smeared into others.
    \item Still \textbf{before-running} and \textbf{label-free} (a property of the picture, not of SAM\,3's output).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- familiarity result
\begin{frame}{\dots and the answer is still no}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item Tried three ways to measure ``distinctness''; ran it on \textbf{SA-FARI and BURST}.
    \item \textbf{None predicts transfer} at the strict bar. And all three turn out to be \textbf{63--85\% the
          same number as the old visual distance} --- they just re-measure ``how different does it look,''
          already a dead end (and a size confound).
    \item On the powered SA-FARI cells the animal-size control still validates --- so this is a \textbf{genuine
          no}, not a weak test.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{block}{Why it matters anyway}
    \centering\footnotesize
    It \textbf{closes the loophole}: even reading familiarity \emph{from the model itself} --- sidestepping the
    ``you don't know its training data'' objection --- you \emph{still} can't forecast its transfer.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- bottom line

  \begin{block}{}\centering\large
    The negative result is now \textbf{robust and triangulated} --- one honest ``no,'' the same across three
    datasets and a model-internal check.
  \end{block}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{itemize}\footnotesize
    \item Powered enough (SA-FARI, BURST) to rule out a moderate effect; the small-sample caveats are stated

    \item Optional next steps --- none of which change the answer --- a larger mask-native dataset for even more

  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
