\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} interpreted the results and set their bounds. This chapter returns to the
problem, states what the evidence answers, what the field gains from that answer, and what remains open.

\section{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:concl-summary}
A practitioner opening a new camera-trap site cannot currently tell whether a promptable tracker will work
on the animals living there. This dissertation asked whether that judgement can be made in advance, from
properties measurable without running the model or labelling its output --- separately for finding the animal
and for following it.

The answer is no, and it survives every criterion applied to it. No distance's coefficient interval excludes
zero; none beats a mean predictor out of sample, on either a species or a location hold-out; together the
four explain about $3\%$ of the variation in detection. The null holds when the encoder, the crop, the prompt
and the distance functional are each swapped in turn; for a second family of label-free properties, intrinsic
scene difficulty, whose apparent effect collapses into animal size; when familiarity is read from the
tracker's own feature space instead of an external reference; on a second, independent dataset; and on two
architecturally distinct trackers substituted for SAM\,3. And it is not a failure of power: the same
procedure detects animal size out of sample wherever size is present.

Only that one property predicts detection, and only modestly --- the nuisance the analysis introduced in
order to control for it. Association carries almost no variance distinct from detection here, so the
decomposition turned out to be a detection story. What remains is a rigorous, decomposed, cross-dataset and
cross-model null.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:concl-contributions}
To our knowledge this is the first out-of-sample test of whether a promptable video tracker's zero-shot
transfer is predictable from label-free, before-running signals, and the first to decompose that question
into detection and association. What the field gains is threefold. The formulation and protocol are reusable: any future before-running
estimator can be held to the same whole-group hold-out bar these well-motivated distances failed. The
explanation is specific: the apparent signals reduce to an animal-size confound, so any future study measuring
visual novelty across species must control for apparent size or it will rediscover the same artefact. And the
caution is practical: proximity to familiar training data is not a safety guarantee, so reliability has to be
checked rather than assumed.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:concl-future}
Three directions remain open. The first is a fuller representational probe: the familiarity proxy shows
that SAM\,3's features separate the species only by re-encoding the visual distance
(Section~\ref{sec:familiarity}); what they encode --- whether taxonomy is represented structurally --- is
untested, as is a proxy anchored on the true, undisclosed pretraining corpus. The second is a broader swap: more trackers, and a larger fair dataset that is no tracker's home turf
(Section~\ref{sec:model_swap}). The third is richer association structure: crowded, multi-object footage
would give the decomposition considerably more to distinguish.
