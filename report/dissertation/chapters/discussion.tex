\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reported what the measurements show; this chapter asks what they mean:
interpretation, reconciliation with prior positives, practical consequences, and the bounds of the claim.

\section{What the result means}
\label{sec:disc-meaning}
Neither hypothesis is supported: H1 and H2 fail the criteria set in advance
(Section~\ref{sec:meth-success}) on both hold-out schemes and for both targets
(Section~\ref{sec:headline}). H0 is what the data support:
transfer is not distance-driven. The manner of failure is as informative as the failure itself: the one
coefficient that reached significance had the opposite sign to that predicted and was not robust to a size
control, and the subsequent difficulty analysis produced the same pattern, its apparent effect attributable
entirely to animal size. The only label-free property that predicts detection is therefore the quantity
introduced as a nuisance covariate.

The detection--association decomposition, the intellectual core of the design, produced a second, subsidiary
finding: association is a near-degenerate target on this data. Of the $346$ positive cells, $268$ ($77\%$)
hold at most one annotated animal per video, so there is only one identity to keep. Across all positive cells
\texttt{pAssA} correlates with \texttt{pDetA} at $0.97$ and is exactly equal to it in $73\%$, leaving little
identity-specific variance for any feature to predict. The decomposition therefore reduces to a detection
analysis, and the association null is reported with those divergence figures as its explanation rather than
as a separate negative result.

\subsection{Novelty at the extremes versus graded prediction}
\label{sec:disc-coarse}
One objection is that a familiar species is detected better than a highly dissimilar one, which appears
inconsistent with a null novelty distance. Both statements are true, because they concern different
resolutions of the same axis. The coarse claim, that the extreme tails differ, is not disputed. The null
reported here concerns the far stronger graded claim that H1 actually tests. That claim is that a continuous
distance predicts where on the detection scale an unseen species lands, out of sample, beyond the size
confound, and well enough to gate a deployment.

The two diverge because the informative variance lies in the middle of the range rather than at the
extremes, which are already predicted by the mean. A coarse familiar-versus-dissimilar signal therefore adds
no information precisely where prediction would be valuable. A genuine extreme-tail effect can coexist with a null graded predictor, and it is the graded,
held-out, size-controlled predictor that a practitioner would need.

\section{Why the null does not contradict prior positives}
\label{sec:disc-related}
The result sits against two literatures of positive findings: one that predicts model performance without
labels, and one that shows backgrounds drive camera-trap failure. Neither is contradicted here.

\subsection{Label-free performance estimation}
Two axes separate this study from every positive result in Section~\ref{sec:bg-predictable}.

The first is the signal: those estimators all read the model's own behaviour on the target, after running
it, whereas this work forecasts from external distances known before a single frame is processed. That
regime is strictly harder, and more useful to a practitioner deciding whether to spend compute.

The second is the output: every predecessor targets a scalar classifier accuracy or the quality of a static
detector or segmenter, and none concerns identity maintained over time or decomposes its estimate. That is
the gap identified in Section~\ref{sec:bg-gap}.

AutoEval~\cite{autoeval} is the closest methodological precedent, and the strength of its positive result
clarifies the contrast: a near-perfect rank correlation between a Fr\'echet distribution-shift distance and
classifier accuracy. It is computed, however, from the model's own features after the target images pass
through the network. We tested AutoEval's exact functional in our regime, Fr\'echet and MMD distances
on cached DINOv2 embeddings computed before running SAM\,3, and it stayed null
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}). The null is not an artefact of the simpler nearest-prototype formulation; the
closest analogue performs no better, running in the opposite direction and losing significance under a size
control.

One comparison makes the boundary concrete: Accuracy-on-the-Line~\cite{aotl} was validated on iWildCam, the
same camera-trap shift this dissertation cites through WILDS and Terra Incognita~\cite{wilds,terra}. The exact
wildlife shift where a classifier's out-of-distribution accuracy is predictable label-free is where our
before-running distances are not.

\subsection{Background bias}
The environment axis inherits its foreground/background separation from a literature that finds backgrounds
to matter substantially (Section~\ref{sec:bg-cameratrap}), so its null requires reconciling; two
distinctions resolve the tension.

The first is causal reliance versus predictive distance. \citet{xiao}, \citet{elephant} and
PanAf-FGBG~\cite{panaf} all intervene on the background and measure the change in performance, a statement
about how a model uses context. Ours is the strictly weaker question of whether a scalar scene
distance forecasts an untouched model's score, and a model may depend causally on context while remaining
unpredictable from such a distance. The null identifies a regime in which distance-based prediction fails; it
does not deny the causal findings.

The second is why the promptable regime should be that regime: those models must infer the category, so a
spuriously correlated background is a usable cue, whereas SAM\,3 is given the category explicitly and the
prompt removes the advantage of relying on scene context. That is the direction \citet{xiao}'s own finding predicts:
models less reliant on such cues close the background gap. The claim is therefore bounded:
background distance does not predict a promptable tracker's detection transfer, which is not a blanket claim
that background is irrelevant.

\section{What this means in practice}
\label{sec:disc-practice}
The field problem that motivated this work --- telling a practitioner, before spending compute, whether a
promptable tracker is trustworthy for a new species and place --- is not solved by label-free distances. The
answer is negative but useful. The intuition that far-from-training implies unreliable does not hold here, so
a deployment gate cannot be built from label-free distance to a reference set. The one predictive label-free
signal, animal size, is too coarse and too weakly informative to serve as one.

One after-running signal does satisfy that criterion, and it is reported explicitly. If a practitioner is willing to run the tracker first, an ATC-style estimator~\cite{atc} reads
SAM\,3's own detection confidence on the target and thresholds it. That predicts detection out of sample on
both schemes, where every label-free distance failed. It is kept outside the contribution for
three reasons. It is after-running, a weaker operating point than the before-running question
posed here, and it is confirmatory of an established family rather than novel. It
is also mechanistically close to circular, since feature and target are both functions of SAM\,3's own
output: a cell with no confident masklet trivially has zero detection accuracy. It is a validated estimator
for the after-running, before-labelling operating point, but not the before-running predictor this
dissertation asks for, nor part of its null.

\section{Limitations, threats to validity, and ethics}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The reference is not the pretraining corpus.} Distances are measured against the SA-FARI train
split, not against SAM\,3's true pretraining data, which is undisclosed; the null is scoped accordingly.
One check was performed: the familiarity proxy derives separability from SAM\,3's own features
(Section~\ref{sec:familiarity}) and is also null, recovering the same size-entangled visual signal; a
coverage-anchored proxy for the actual corpus remains untested. As implemented, the visual, environment and
size features also read the benchmark's ground-truth masks to separate animal from scene
(Section~\ref{sec:meth-features}); this is a benchmark convenience, since in deployment the species
prototype would be built from a practitioner's exemplar images.

\paragraph{One dataset and one backbone.} A single inference pass is reused across both splits, so the
species and location experiments are overlays on the same scored cells rather than independent replications. Against the dataset threat, the whole pipeline was re-run on an independent dataset, where the
null holds again (Section~\ref{sec:burst}): a second convergent null, not positive proof, since that dataset
remains underpowered for a small species-constant effect. Against the model threat, the controlled
swap put two architecturally distinct trackers through the same procedure with the same result
(Section~\ref{sec:model_swap}). A third dataset, MammAlps~\cite{mammalps}, was run in an earlier pass and
is directionally consistent with the null, but is not counted here: at roughly $20$ cells over $5$ species it
is too small to certify one, and its per-clip annotations are not reproducible from the public release.

\paragraph{The effective sample is groups, not cells.} The sample comprises tens of independent species and
locations, not hundreds of cells; inference is therefore group-clustered, and aggregate correlations are
reported alongside their weaker cell-level values to avoid the ecological-correlation fallacy. Representational probing is likewise only partly addressed: the familiarity proxy shows the
features separate the species, but not what they encode (Section~\ref{sec:concl-future}).

\paragraph{The scorer.} All scores come from the unmodified official evaluator, which wraps standard
HOTA-family metrics; its one SAM\,3-calibrated parameter, a fixed $0.5$ confidence gate, does not determine
the cross-model comparison (Section~\ref{sec:model_swap}).

\paragraph{Ethical considerations.}
The data are anonymised camera-trap footage under a non-commercial licence.
Because label-free distance does not reliably indicate failure, low distance from training data is no safety
guarantee; reliability should be checked with labelled spot-audits, not assumed.
