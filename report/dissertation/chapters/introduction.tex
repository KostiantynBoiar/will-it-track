\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

\section{Motivation}
Promptable video trackers --- of which SAM\,3~\cite{sam3} is the current state of the art --- segment and
track any named animal in a video with no training on that species, and conservation teams now deploy them on
new species and regions constantly. Their appeal is exactly this zero-shot reach; their liability is that it is
\emph{unpredictable}, varying sharply from one animal or place to the next. Yet no rule tells a practitioner,
\emph{before running the model}, whether its output is trustworthy for a given animal in a given place --- so a
team pointing a tracker at a new species cannot tell in advance whether to trust it or whether the costly
alternative, manual annotation, is still required. This dissertation studies that gap on the leading such tracker
(SAM\,3), and then asks whether whatever we find is specific to it.

\section{Problem statement and research question}
This dissertation asks whether a promptable tracker's zero-shot accuracy on an unseen (species, place) can be
\emph{predicted in advance} from properties measurable without any label of the target, and which factors govern
that transfer --- separately for \emph{finding} the animal and \emph{following} it. We study this concretely on
SAM\,3 as the primary case, measuring novelty against the SA-FARI reference split (Section~\ref{sec:bg-safari}),
and then test whether the answer generalises beyond one dataset and one model --- to two further datasets and,
via a controlled model swap, to two other promptable trackers (Section~\ref{sec:model_swap}).

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6em,style=nextline]
  \item[H0 (null)] the label-free distances carry no predictive information about
    \texttt{pDetA}/\texttt{pAssA} --- transfer is not distance-driven.
  \item[H1 (detection)] \texttt{pDetA} falls with species novelty (taxonomic + visual distance).
  \item[H2 (association)] \texttt{pAssA} falls with environment difficulty (scene, day/night/IR,
    clutter, camera motion).
\end{description}
Failing to reject H0 is itself an informative result --- and, as Chapter~\ref{ch:results} shows, it is
what we find: neither a novelty distance nor a follow-up \emph{intrinsic-difficulty} pivot predicts
transfer beyond a trivial animal-size effect. Representational probing (does SAM\,3 encode taxonomy?)
remains the open branch for future work.

\section{Contributions}
To our knowledge this is the first study to \emph{predict}, before the model is run and from label-free
distances, the zero-shot transfer of a promptable video tracker --- and the first to split that prediction
into \textbf{detection} (\texttt{pDetA}) and \textbf{association} (\texttt{pAssA}). This distinguishes it
from label-free performance prediction for classifiers, which estimates a single scalar from the model's own
outputs on the target~\cite{aotl,agl,atc}, and from the very recent static-image detector/segmenter
estimators~\cite{pcr,rca}; and from camera-trap shift studies, which measure but do not forecast per-site
reliability~\cite{terra,panaf,jeon}. Concretely we deliver:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.4em]
  \item a rigorous, out-of-sample \emph{negative} result --- under a deliberately powerful test (whole-species
    and whole-location hold-out with group-cluster-bootstrap significance), label-free before-running distances
    do \emph{not} predict SAM\,3's transfer;
  \item the detection-versus-association decomposition, tested on two near-orthogonal splits --- a constructed
    species hold-out (novelty) and the official location hold-out (environment);
  \item a \emph{hardened} account of \emph{why} it fails: a follow-up intrinsic-difficulty pivot collapses to the
    same animal-size confound, so the only label-free property that predicts detection is object size --- the
    very nuisance the analysis controls for --- and that positive control confirms the test detects signal when
    it exists;
  \item evidence that the null is not an artefact of \emph{one} dataset or \emph{one} model: it holds across
    three datasets (SA-FARI, MammAlps, BURST) and, in a controlled model swap on BURST, across two
    architecturally distinct promptable trackers (GLEE and Florence-2\,+\,SAM\,2), so it is task-general rather
    than a quirk of SAM\,3.
\end{enumerate}
